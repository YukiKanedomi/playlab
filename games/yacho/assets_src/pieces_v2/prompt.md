# Task

Generate TWO separate images using the image generation tool, one at a time.
Each image is a single sheet containing 5 game pieces in a horizontal row for
a mobile matching-board game. Follow the full prompt text for each image
exactly as written below. Save the first as `./pieces_v2_a.png` and the
second as `./pieces_v2_b.png`.

Minor stylistic imperfection is acceptable; do not spend extra turns
correcting small issues.

## IMAGE A — save as pieces_v2_a.png

A single reference sheet for a mobile matching-board game: five distinct
game piece types laid out in a horizontal row, each occupying its own
square cell of equal size, evenly spaced left to right. This is drawn as a
naturalist's specimen-catalogue plate.

Transparent watercolour washes over a fine ink line, on cold-press paper.
The brightest passages are bare unpainted paper — warm cream, not white —
and highlights stay between 15 and 22 percent saturation. Saturation peaks
in the midtones around 28 to 36 percent and falls away toward the light.
The darkest passage keeps 20 to 35 percent saturation and never drops below
luminance 22: there is no black and no neutral grey anywhere.
Shadows leave the hue of the light. Lit surfaces sit in warm ochre around
hue 35 to 45 degrees, and shadow falls to a single destination chosen for
the setting: olive around hue 65 to 85 for vegetation, blue-green around
hue 150 to 170 for wet stone and oxidised metal, slate blue around hue 210
to 230 for cold interiors.
The contour is a fine, slightly wobbly warm charcoal line near #2F2922 at
about 27 percent saturation — a near-neutral warm grey, not a saturated
brown. It swells on the underside and at overlaps and thins away to nothing
along the top-lit edge, and pigment pools in a hairline just inside it, no
wider than the contour itself, so the edge reads darker than the body.
Every form carries two kinds of edge at once: a hard dry edge where a wash
stops abruptly against the ink line, and one or two soft wet-in-wet blooms
inside the form.
The tooth of the cold-press paper and the granulation of the pigment stay
visible across every flat area, interface panels included; no surface is
perfectly smooth.
Where no wash is laid, darkening is done by drawn line: fine parallel
hatching and stipple in recesses and under overhangs.

The five slots, left to right:

1. A cluster of three to four angular translucent crystal shards fused at a
   common base, the tallest shard in the centre, growing narrower and more
   pointed toward the tips. The facets are hard-edged planes; two adjacent
   facets catch a broken warm-white highlight while the rest sit in
   slate-blue shadow, hue around 210 to 230. Saturation peaks in the
   mid-toned facets and the tips fade toward pale, near-transparent paper.
   A thin vein of duller stone clings to the base where it was broken from
   rock.
2. A single worn brass cog wheel seen face-on, eight uneven teeth around
   its rim, two of them chipped or bent, a round axle hole at the centre.
   The metal is aged brass, hue 34 to 40, with green-blue oxidised patina
   pooling in the tooth roots and around the axle hole. A broken warm-white
   highlight crosses the upper-left of the rim only, stopping where the
   metal is pitted. One tooth is missing entirely, leaving a rough broken
   edge.
3. A single broad ovate leaf with a pointed drip-tip, its central vein and
   four to five side veins drawn as fine ink lines, one edge curling
   slightly and browning at the tip. The leaf sits in warm ochre where lit
   and falls to olive shadow, hue 65 to 85, along its curled edge and where
   the veins recess. A small insect-bite notch interrupts one edge of the
   silhouette.
4. A single domed toadstool cap on a short thick stem, the cap asymmetric
   and slightly tilted rather than a perfect dome, its underside gills just
   visible where the cap tilts up on one side. The cap pigment is a muted
   rust-red related to #A8613C, never a saturated pure red, deepening
   toward the rim and fading to paper cream at the crown where a bloom of
   spores has dusted it pale. The stem is a duller warm grey-brown with
   faint fibrous striations.
5. A single rounded, lumpy stone about the size of a fist, its surface
   patched with lichen and moss in two distinct violet families — a cool
   blue-violet lichen filling the shaded crevices and hollows, and a dusty
   red-violet moss catching the light on the upper facets — so the colour
   never reads as one smooth violet gradient. Bare stone shows through in a
   few worn patches, warm grey-brown, matching the surrounding rock.

Style: each piece is drawn with a marginally firmer, more confident contour
than usual (still variable in weight along its length, never a uniform
single-width line) and is surrounded by generous negative space within its
cell — clean and mostly free of dirt, as if cleaned and set out for study,
like a specimen laid on a museum plate.

Each of the five pieces sits fully within its own conceptual square cell;
the cells are equal width and evenly spaced in a single horizontal row, but
no grid lines, borders or dividers are drawn — the background around and
between every piece is fully transparent. No drop shadow is baked under any
piece.

Avoid: airbrushed continuous gradients and smooth digital shading; a
uniform drop-shadow effect; a continuous bevel-and-specular metal render;
additive gaussian bloom or a glow halo around light sources; blur, fog or
depth-of-field used to push things back; pure black #000000 and pure white
#FFFFFF; saturated highlights above 50 percent saturation; a saturated
brown contour; uniform-width outline strokes; rounded-rectangle cards with
even padding and an even gold hairline; dotted underlines; perfectly
mirrored ornament and evenly spaced rivets; a flawless untouched surface;
rainbow or violet gradients; lens flare, rim light and dramatic backlight;
emoji; any lettering, numerals, logos or name plates.

Output: one image, landscape aspect roughly 5 to 1, five equal square cells
in a single horizontal row. Fully transparent background, PNG with alpha,
all edges and all four corners of the full canvas transparent, and the
transparent gaps between pieces genuinely alpha-zero, not white or pale
paper. No text, no numerals, no letters, no cell borders, no labels of any
kind.
